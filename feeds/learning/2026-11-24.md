RESOURCE_TITLE: Database Internals with Alex Petrov
RESOURCE_URL: https://softwareengineeringdaily.com/2019/10/22/database-internals-with-alex-petrov/
RESOURCE_SOURCE: Software Engineering Daily

WHY_NOW:
As distributed storage engines and specialized databases continue to proliferate in modern cloud architectures, treating your datastore as a black box is a liability. Understanding low-level database mechanics enables you to debug cryptic performance bottlenecks and make informed tradeoffs between read-heavy and write-heavy storage models.

WHAT_YOULL_LEARN:
- The fundamental mechanics and trade-offs between B-Trees and Log-Structured Merge (LSM) Trees.
- How storage engines handle page management, immutability, and write-ahead logs (WAL).
- The interplay between local storage algorithms and distributed consensus protocols like Raft and Paxos.
- How concurrency control, isolation levels, and Multi-Version Concurrency Control (MVCC) function at the storage layer.

WHO_ITS_FOR:
This episode is tailored for backend, systems, and data infrastructure engineers who want to demystify database storage engines. It is particularly valuable for developers designing high-throughput backend services that require fine-grained query and storage optimization.

TIME_INVESTMENT: 52 minutes

BEST_QUOTE_OR_HIGHLIGHT:
"Understanding database storage engines comes down to recognizing the fundamental mechanical sympathy: you are always balancing write amplification, read amplification, and space amplification based on your application's access patterns."

NEXT_STEP:
Audit your application's primary database workload to identify whether its storage engine uses a B-Tree variant or an LSM-Tree, and evaluate if your access patterns align with that engine's read/write amplification design.
