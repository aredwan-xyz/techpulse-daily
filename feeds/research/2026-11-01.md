## 🎯 The Core Idea
The researchers debunked a complex theory that claimed adding random noise to an AI’s internal weights uncovers hidden "expert" models. Instead, they show that simply adding random noise to the model's final output scores achieves the exact same reasoning boost with far less effort.

## 🔍 The Problem They Solved
A recent popular hypothesis suggested that language models hide dense clusters of task experts deep within their weight geometry, requiring compute-heavy modifications to internal parameters to unlock them. This led engineers to believe that complex, full-model weight manipulation was necessary to squeeze out extra reasoning performance.

## 💡 The Key Innovation
The authors proved that these gains have nothing to do with deep weight magic, but rather with basic ensembling: generating varied predictions, picking the best candidates on a small test set, and letting them vote. In fact, leaving the model completely frozen and lightly shaking up the final output predictions (the logits) matched or beat modifying the actual weights across multiple reasoning tasks. They formalized this lightweight approach into a simple recipe called **Perturb-Select-Vote (PSV)**.

## 🛠️ Why Developers Should Care
You don’t need to clone massive model checkpoints in GPU memory or hack internal weights to get the accuracy boost of model ensembling. You can get identical performance gains purely through black-box techniques by injecting slight randomness into the final outputs of a frozen model via standard APIs.

Perturbing a single decoder block already recovers much of the improvement, while keeping the model entirely frozen and adding a random bias to its output logits matches or exceeds full-weight perturbations on most reasoning tasks. This suggests an alternative explanation based on output diversity, validation-based selection, and voting.
