## 🎯 The Core Idea
The researchers built "Scientist World," a platform that automatically turns academic papers into interactive, runnable software environments. Inside these environments, AI agents are tasked with solving the paper's core research question and outperforming the original human authors' codebase.

## 🔍 The Problem They Solved
While AI agents can easily *read* PDF research papers, they couldn't actually *run* them as interactive sandboxes to practice doing science. Without executable environments, developers had no realistic way to evaluate whether AI agents can genuinely conduct research and improve existing codebases.

## 💡 The Key Innovation
The authors created an automated pipeline that takes a research paper, extracts its underlying problem, and sets up the author's method as a baseline environment. They released 1,000 of these verified, executable environments to act as a standardized gym for AI scientists. Using this benchmark, they discovered that state-of-the-art AI agents can actually beat the original human researchers' results

## 📝 Original Abstract

> AI-Scientists arrived, but a world for them to grow in did not. Human scientists take theirs for granted $-$ centuries of open problems and shared ideas, waiting in the scientific literature. To AI-Scientists, that world is readable but not runnable, so we build Scientist World for them: every research paper becomes an executable environment. Our autonomous pipeline extracts each paper's open-ended research question and reproduces its proposed method as the baseline; an AI-Scientist entering the environment is evaluated against what the human authors achieved. We release the first 1,000 verified environments. With the world as an evaluation, we found that frontier AI-Scientists can already surpass the human researchers 74% of the time.
